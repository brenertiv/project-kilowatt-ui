# Treemap

A treemap shows how one whole splits into parts as rectangles, each sized by its value. `Treemap` is Kilowatt's reference implementation: amCharts 5's hierarchy treemap (`@amcharts/amcharts5/hierarchy`) run through `useChart`, so it takes the chart theme for the active color scheme (Rules → Charts). It's one level deep: the parts of one whole, with no drilling in. Enertiv's app rebuilds its charts from this one rather than importing it.

<Canvas of={TreemapStories.Parts} />

## When to use

- Use a treemap for the parts of one whole where the card is wide and short, such as a fact card on a dashboard: floor area by property type, cost by utility. Its rectangles fill the space a donut would leave empty.
- For the same parts where the card is square, or the share matters more than the size, use a donut chart.
- For parts that change over time, use a stacked column chart.
- For comparing values that don't add up to a whole, use a column chart.
- For nested groups (a region, then its properties) or values people look up, use a table; this treemap is one level deep and groups past six.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Treemap } from 'src/charts/Treemap';

<Card variant="outlined">
  <CardHeader title="Floor Area by Property Type" />
  <Treemap data={floorArea} unit="sq ft" summary="Office is 51% of the portfolio's floor area." />
</Card>;
```

`data` is a list of `{ category, value }`, in any order. Keep it stable (a constant or `useMemo`), since the chart is rebuilt when it changes. `height` defaults to 240px.

## Variants

- **Parts.** Up to six rectangles, largest first, in `chart.series` order, each labeled with its name and share where it fits.
- **Grouped into Other.** With more than six parts, the five largest keep their rectangles and the rest are summed into one in `chart.neutral`, named by `otherLabel`. Say in the summary what "Other" holds.
- **Meanings.** Parts that stand for a product meaning (a work-order type, a utility) take their `slot`: spread `meaningColor(workOrderTypeSlots, type)` into each part, keyed on the API's name, so the meaning has one color on every chart. Parts without a slot take the free ones, largest first.

## Do and don't

- Do put the chart in an outlined `Card` (`variant="outlined"`) under a title-only `CardHeader` that says what's measured, straight in the `Card` with no `CardContent`: the chart pads itself through `inset`, 16px by default, `1` (8px) for a tighter fit, `0` to run edge to edge. The page sets the period.
- Don't put the unit in the card header or a subtitle; `unit` puts it in the tooltip, next to each value, and the summary should state it with its numbers.
- Do use a treemap only for parts that add up to a meaningful whole.
- Don't use a treemap for values that overlap or don't sum, such as averages or rates; use a column chart.
- Do let the chart group the smallest parts into "Other" past six, and name them in the summary.
- Don't raise the part count or add colors to fit more parts; use a table instead.
- Do give parts that stand for product meanings their `slot` from `src/charts/meanings`.
- Don't nest a second level into the rectangles; show the breakdown of one part in its own chart or a table.
- Do write `summary` as one sentence with the share that matters.
- Don't add a legend; each rectangle is labeled directly, and a label that doesn't fit is hidden rather than shrunk, with the part still named in its tooltip.

## MUI departures

- `chart-palette`: rectangles take `chart.series` in order, largest first, or by `slot`; the grouped rectangle takes `chart.neutral`.

Theme-wide departures (the surface levels the card sits on, the theme radius) apply too; see Foundations → MUI departures.

## amCharts departures

How this chart differs from stock amCharts 5, from `src/charts/departures.ts`; Foundations → amCharts departures has the reasons.

- `chart-colors`: the chart's chrome takes the palette's roles, and parts take `chart.series`, used as they are. A rectangle's label is in the card surface color (amCharts' `alternativeText`, which the theme maps to the card), which passes 3:1 on every series color in both schemes.
- `chart-type`: labels are in the theme's font at the `caption` size.
- `chart-tooltips`: tooltips sit on the menu surface with `text.primary`, outlined and rounded like a menu.
- `reduced-motion`: the chart animates only when the user hasn't asked for reduced motion.
- `chart-card`: the chart sits straight in an outlined `Card` under a title-only `CardHeader` and pads itself 16px (`inset`).
- `full-value-tooltips`: every rectangle has a tooltip with its exact value, unit and share (`tooltipNumberFormat`).
- `chart-text-alternative`: the `figure` around the chart is named by `summary` (`aria-label`).

## Accessibility

- Every rectangle shows its name, exact value, unit and share in a tooltip on hover, including the ones too small for a label.
- The chart draws on a canvas, so the `figure` around it is named by `summary` through `aria-label`. The summary isn't shown on screen; it's the chart's text alternative.
- Parts are told apart by their direct labels (name and share) and their size, as well as color.
- For exact values, offer the data as a table beside the chart.
