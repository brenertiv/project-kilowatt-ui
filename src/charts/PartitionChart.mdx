# Partition chart

A partition chart, or icicle, shows how a whole splits into parts and how each part splits again: a building's energy use by system, then by equipment. `PartitionChart` is Kilowatt's reference implementation: amCharts 5's hierarchy partition (`@amcharts/amcharts5/hierarchy`) run through `useChart`, so it takes the chart theme for the active color scheme (Rules → Charts), inside MUI's `Card` (Material UI v9). The whole is a column at the left and each level sits a column further right, every rectangle as tall as its share; a branch keeps one color through its levels. Enertiv's app rebuilds its charts from this one rather than importing it.

## When to use

- Use a partition chart for a hierarchy two or three levels deep whose values add up at every level: a building, its systems and their equipment; a portfolio, its regions and their properties.
- For one level of parts, use a treemap in a wide, short card or a donut in a square one.
- For parts that change over time, use a stacked column chart.
- For a deep or wide hierarchy, or values people look up, use a table with expandable rows.

## Usage

```tsx
import Card from '@mui/material/Card';
import CardHeader from '@mui/material/CardHeader';
import { PartitionChart } from 'src/charts/PartitionChart';

const energy = {
  name: '350 Park Avenue',
  children: [
    { name: 'HVAC', children: [{ name: 'Chillers', value: 9840 }, { name: 'Air handlers', value: 6120 }] },
    { name: 'Lighting', children: [{ name: 'Tenant floors', value: 5210 }, { name: 'Common areas', value: 2340 }] },
    { name: 'Domestic hot water', value: 1260 },
  ],
};

<Card variant="outlined">
  <CardHeader title="Energy Use by System" />
  <PartitionChart data={energy} unit="kWh" summary="HVAC was 64% of the building's energy use, led by the chillers." />
</Card>;
```

Props, with their defaults:

- `data`: the root node, `{ name, children }`; each child is a leaf `{ name, value }` or a group with its own `children`. A branch (a child of the root) may take a `slot` or `neutral` (`SeriesColor`). Keep it stable (a constant or `useMemo`).
- `unit`: after each value in the tooltip.
- `summary`: one sentence, the figure's accessible name.
- `height`: the chart's height in pixels. Default `320`.
- `inset`: the padding at the sides and bottom, `2` (16px), `1` (8px) or `0`. Default `2`.

## Variants

- **Building.** Three columns: the whole in `chart.neutral`, its branches in `chart.series` order, and each branch's parts in their branch's color. A click on a group zooms into it; a click on the column at its left zooms back out.
- **One level.** A root with only leaves: each is its own branch and color. For this alone a treemap or a donut usually reads better; it is here for a hierarchy that happens to be shallow.

## Do and don't

- Do put the chart in an outlined `Card` (`variant="outlined"`) under a title-only `CardHeader` that says what's measured, straight in the `Card` with no `CardContent`: the chart pads itself through `inset`. The page sets the period.
- Don't put the unit in the card header or a subtitle; `unit` puts it in the tooltip, next to each value, and the summary should state it with its numbers.
- Do use it only where every level's values add up to the level above.
- Don't use it for values that overlap or don't sum, such as averages or rates.
- Do keep to six branches, the number of series colors; group the smallest into an Other branch in your data.
- Don't give each node its own color; a branch's parts share its color so color names the branch.
- Do order branches and their children largest first in `data`.
- Don't rely on the zoom to show small parts; name what matters in `summary`, and offer a table for the rest.

## MUI departures

- `chart-palette`: branches take `chart.series` in order or by `slot`, and the whole takes `chart.neutral`.

Theme-wide departures (the surface levels the card sits on, the theme radius) apply too; see Foundations → MUI departures.

## amCharts departures

How this chart differs from stock amCharts 5, from `src/charts/departures.ts`; Foundations → amCharts departures has the reasons.

- `chart-colors`: the chart's chrome takes the palette's roles, and branches take `chart.series`, used as they are.
- `chart-type`: labels are in the theme's font at the `caption` size, in `text.secondary`.
- `chart-tooltips`: tooltips sit on the menu surface with `text.primary`, outlined and rounded like a menu.
- `reduced-motion`: the chart animates only when the user hasn't asked for reduced motion.
- `chart-card`: the chart sits straight in an outlined `Card` under a title-only `CardHeader` and pads itself 16px (`inset`).
- `full-value-tooltips`: every rectangle has a tooltip with its name, exact value, unit and share of the whole (`tooltipNumberFormat`).
- `number-separators`: a tooltip's exact value takes the viewer's thousands and decimal separators, as the axis numbers do.
- `chart-text-alternative`: the `figure` around the chart is named by `summary` (`aria-label`).
- `branch-colors`: each branch keeps one series color through its levels and the whole is `chart.neutral`; labels take the palette's contrast text and are hidden where they don't fit.

## Accessibility

- Every rectangle shows its name, exact value, unit and share of the whole in a tooltip on hover, including the ones too small for a label.
- The chart draws on a canvas, so the `figure` around it is named by `summary` through `aria-label`. Say the largest branch and its share.
- Rectangles are told apart by their labels, position and size as well as color; each label takes the palette's contrast text for its rectangle.
- Zooming into a branch is by pointer only. Offer the same hierarchy as a table with expandable rows beside the chart.
